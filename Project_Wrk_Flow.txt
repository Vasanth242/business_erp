# Business Management System – Customer Walkthrough

## 1. Dashboard – Overall Business View

The **Dashboard** is the main overview of the business. It gives management a quick picture of what is happening across the system without having to open each individual module.

The dashboard provides information such as:

* **Total Sales**
* **Total Purchases**
* **Total Expenses**
* **Current Inventory**
* **Customer Outstanding**
* **Business Trends**
* **Recent Transactions**

An important point is that these dashboard figures are **not manually entered**.

They are automatically derived from the transactions recorded in the respective sections of the system.

For example:

* Purchases recorded in the Purchase module contribute to purchase totals.
* Completed sales contribute to sales totals.
* Completed expenses contribute to expense totals.
* Purchase and sales transactions affect inventory.
* Customer payments affect outstanding balances.

This means the dashboard represents the actual business activity recorded in the system.

### Business Trends

The dashboard also allows users to change the reporting period.

The current system supports:

* Last 7 days
* Last 30 days
* Last 12 months

When the period is changed, the dashboard reloads the corresponding business information for that period.

### Customer Outstanding

The dashboard also provides visibility into the amount currently pending from customers.

This allows management to quickly understand how much money is still receivable without having to check every customer or invoice individually.

---

# 2. Master Data

Before recording daily business transactions, the system maintains the basic information required to operate the business.

This information acts as the foundation for the other modules.

## Products

The **Products** section is used to maintain all products that the business deals with.

Product information can then be selected when recording purchases and sales.

## Customers

The **Customers** section is used to maintain customer information.

Customers are then associated with sales transactions, invoices, payments and outstanding balances.

## Suppliers

The **Suppliers** section is used to maintain supplier information.

Suppliers are associated with purchase transactions and supplier payments.

## Locations

The **Locations** section is used to maintain the different locations where stock is stored.

This allows inventory to be associated with the appropriate stock location.

### Why Master Data Is Important

Products, customers, suppliers and locations are the **basic building blocks of the system**.

Once this information is maintained, users can reuse it while recording purchases, sales, payments and other business transactions instead of entering the same information repeatedly.

---

# 3. Purchase Management

Let's consider a real business example.

Suppose the business receives **100 units of a product from a supplier**.

The user can create a purchase by selecting:

* Supplier
* Invoice details
* Stock location
* Product
* Quantity

The purchase is then saved in the system.

However, saving the purchase is different from completing the business transaction.

The important business action happens when the purchase is **completed**.

Once the purchase is completed, the system automatically updates the stock.

The user does **not** need to separately open Inventory and manually add the purchased quantity.

This is one of the key integrations within the system.

---

# 4. Inventory Integration

Immediately after completing the purchase, the Inventory module reflects the change.

For example:

**Before Purchase**

Stock = 500 units

**Purchase Completed**

+100 units

**After Purchase**

Stock = 600 units

This demonstrates that inventory is not maintained as a completely separate manual process.

Instead, inventory reflects the actual business transactions recorded in the system.

In other words:

**Purchase completed → Stock automatically increases**

---

# 5. Sales Management

Now consider the opposite business activity.

Suppose the business sells **20 units** of the same product to a customer.

The user selects:

* Customer
* Product
* Quantity

and completes the sale.

Once the sale is completed, the system automatically reduces the available stock.

Using the previous example:

**Available Stock**

600 units

**Sale**

-20 units

**Remaining Stock**

580 units

This demonstrates the connection between **Sales and Inventory**.

The user does not need to manually reduce inventory after completing the sale.

The transaction itself drives the inventory change.

---

# 6. Customer Invoice and Outstanding

The sale also creates the customer's transaction/invoice.

This allows the business to track whether the customer has:

* Fully paid
* Partially paid
* Not paid yet

The system can therefore maintain the customer's outstanding amount based on the payments recorded against the invoice.

This is particularly useful for businesses that sell goods on credit or allow customers to make payments after the sale.

---

# 7. Customer Payments

Now suppose the customer makes a payment of **₹30,000**.

The user records the payment against the customer and completes the payment transaction.

The payment can then be allocated against the relevant customer invoice.

For example:

| Transaction      |  Amount |
| ---------------- | ------: |
| Invoice          | ₹50,000 |
| Customer Payment | ₹30,000 |
| Outstanding      | ₹20,000 |

The system identifies that the invoice is **partially paid** and that **₹20,000 remains outstanding**.

The invoice status is automatically determined based on the completed payment allocations:

* **UNPAID**
* **PARTIALLY_PAID**
* **PAID**

The user therefore does not have to manually maintain the invoice payment status.

---

# 8. Completing the Customer Payment

Suppose the customer later pays the remaining **₹20,000**.

The payment is recorded and allocated against the invoice.

The result becomes:

| Transaction |  Amount |
| ----------- | ------: |
| Invoice     | ₹50,000 |
| Total Paid  | ₹50,000 |
| Outstanding |      ₹0 |
| Status      |    Paid |

The system automatically recognizes that the invoice has now been fully paid.

This means the business does not need to maintain a separate manual record of which invoices are partially paid or fully paid.

The payment transactions determine the invoice status.

The customer's outstanding balance is also updated accordingly and reflected in the relevant dashboard information.

---

# 9. Supplier Payments

The system also handles the other side of the business: **payments to suppliers**.

The basic flow is:

**Supplier → Purchase → Amount Payable → Supplier Payment**

This allows the business to record purchases from suppliers and subsequently track the payments made to those suppliers.

The same transaction-based approach is used here: business information is recorded once and the relevant areas of the system are updated based on those transactions.

---

# 10. Expense Management

Apart from purchasing goods, a business also has regular operating expenses.

These can be recorded separately in the **Expenses** section.

Typical examples include:

* Transport
* Rent
* Maintenance
* Utilities
* Other business expenses

Once an expense is completed, it contributes to the overall expense information shown in the dashboard.

Therefore:

**Expense completed → Expense totals increase → Dashboard reflects the change**

# 11. Returning to the Dashboard

After demonstrating purchases, sales, inventory, payments and expenses, we can return to the Dashboard.

At this point, the dashboard provides a consolidated view of the business activity.

Management can see information such as:

* Sales
* Purchases
* Expenses
* Inventory
* Customer Payments
* Customer Outstanding
* Recent Transactions
* Business Trends

The purpose of the dashboard is to ensure that management does not have to open every individual module to understand what is happening in the business.

It provides a consolidated view based on the transactions entered throughout the system.

# 13. The Core Business Concept

The main objective of the system is:

> **The user should enter the business information once, and the system should automatically reflect the impact in the relevant areas.**

For example:

**Purchase entered and completed → Stock increases**

**Sale completed → Stock decreases**

**Payment recorded → Customer outstanding decreases**

**Expense completed → Expense totals increase**

**All relevant transactions → Dashboard information updates**

This transaction-driven approach reduces duplicate data entry and provides a more accurate view of the current business position.

---

# 14. Overall Business Value

The system brings the major business activities together into one connected workflow.

Instead of maintaining separate records for purchases, sales, inventory, customer payments, supplier payments and expenses, the system allows these activities to work together.

The result is a more connected business process where:

* Master data provides the foundation.
* Purchases increase inventory.
* Sales reduce inventory.
* Customer invoices track amounts receivable.
* Customer payments reduce outstanding balances.
* Supplier payments track amounts payable.
* Expenses contribute to business expenses.
* The dashboard consolidates the resulting business information.

The key benefit is **integration**.

The user records the business transaction, and the system automatically reflects its impact across the relevant modules.

This makes the system easier to operate, reduces manual reconciliation, and gives management a clearer picture of the business.
